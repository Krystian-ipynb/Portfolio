66/11 kV receiving substation

Design package for a two-transformer distribution substation in Brisbane: load growth, fault levels, protection settings and a drafted single-line diagram.

  • MATLAB
  • AutoCAD Electrical
  • IEC 60909
  • AS/NZS 2067
FocusPower systems
LocationBrisbane, QLD
Year2025
Transformers2 × 40 MVA, Dyn11

The problem

A growing 11 kV load needs a new 66/11 kV substation that keeps every feeder supplied if one transformer is lost, fits an 80 m × 60 m site and meets Energex network connection requirements.

Worth knowing

  • 66 kV bus-section breaker is normally open and closes automatically when a transformer is lost.
  • 11 kV solar bus-section breaker is normally closed and opens on a solar trip (anti-islanding).
  • Vector group corrected to Dyn11 in Rev D.
  • Short-circuit rating of 31.5 kA at 66 kV and 25 kA at 11 kV for 3 seconds, with resistance earthing limited to 400 A.
  • A 27-item equipment schedule covering switchgear, instrument transformers, PLCC and protection relays (50/51, 27/59, 59N, 51N/87N, 87T).
  • Includes a 10 MW solar farm feeder, with SCADA over IEC 61850 and DNP3.
  • One MATLAB script runs 13 parts, from load analysis to the equipment schedule.

How it works

  • Projected load over 20 years and sized two transformers so each can carry 100% of peak demand (N-1).
  • Calculated fault levels to IEC 60909 in per-unit with both transformers in parallel, then checked circuit breaker ratings against them.
  • Selected CT ratios, verified on-load tap changer voltage regulation, and sized the busbars (AS/NZS 2067) and four 11 kV feeders.
  • Wrote a protection settings schedule and sized the neutral earthing resistor.
  • Added PLCC equipment, a solar incomer with its own bus-section breaker, and drafted everything as a single-line diagram.
